AI language models are fast, cheap, and genuinely useful in R&D workflows. They also fabricate with total confidence, and the fabrications cluster in specific, predictable patterns. This toolkit addresses the gap between knowing that and doing something about it.

What changed from the manifesto: triage (not every claim needs full DOI resolution), cost estimates (time per verification step), checklists (not just principles), and a working ledger (not a static example).

Core rule: No orphan claims. Every assertion is tied to a verified source, a reproducible derivation, or is explicitly labeled as conjecture.

Guide How to use this toolkit

Start with Triage, end with the Ledger

1. Triage Calculator — Classify your claim as P0 (design-critical), P1 (supporting), or P2 (background). This determines verification depth and time budget.

2. Failure Modes — Run through the relevant checklists for your claim type. Check items off as you complete them.

3. Verification Ledger — Log every claim, its source, exact location, and status. Edit in-place. Add rows as you go.

4. Export — Dump the ledger as Markdown or CSV for your paper's supplementary materials.

00Failure Mode

Source laundering

The most common error: a review article cites a benchmark; the model cites the review as though it were the original evidence. Five recent articles all state a nutrition claim confidently — all five trace back to a single 2019 study. You found an echo, not confirmation.

Protocol — Trace to root

For any claim central to a design decision, trace it to its evidentiary root — the original measurement or experiment later citations depend on.

01Failure Mode

Citation hallucination — and the quote that isn't there

A model returns a citation that's subtly wrong — right topic, wrong authors, wrong journal, or a DOI that resolves to an unrelated paper. Or the DOI is correct, but the attributed claim, figure, or number never appears in the paper.

Protocol — DOI-gate, two steps

Step A: Every DOI must independently resolve to the claimed title and authors. Step B: The specific number, quote, or figure must be matched to its exact location — page, table, or figure.

03Failure Mode

The closed-loop trap

A system asked to resolve an open verification item sometimes completes the loop itself — supplying the missing data, marking the item resolved, and presenting the whole thing as settled. It's the system grading its own homework.

Protocol — Closure hierarchy

Whoever raises a concern shouldn't be the one who decides it's resolved. AI-only closure of an AI-raised flag stays provisional, never final.

Claim Triage

Not every claim deserves the same scrutiny. Select the level that matches the claim's role in your argument. This sets the verification budget and required closure authority.
